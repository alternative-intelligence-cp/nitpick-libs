## SUPERSEDED

**1. tzdata 2026c → 2026e (nitpick-time).**
- **2026d** was released 2026-09-11: Canada's Northwest Territories moved to permanent -06 on 2026-08-21, abbreviation CST.
- **2026e** was released 2026-09-29: Manitoba moves to permanent -05 on 2026-10-31, and Ireland's 1925 fall-back is corrected to 09-20.
- Source: https://data.iana.org/time-zones/tzdb/NEWS and https://www.iana.org/time-zones ("Released 2026-09-29").
- What it could change:
  - `TZDB_VERSION` ("2026c") and the "latest at cycle 0.5" pin in TM-100 are one release behind, two counting 2026d.
  - Zone transitions for the Northwest Territories, Manitoba and Ireland change, so the transition and type counts in `tzdb-size.md` (27 183 transitions, 2 513 types) shift slightly.
  - The size margin is 1 418 transitions (about 4.4%). I infer these edits are far smaller than that, but I did not measure it.
  - CURRENCY.md:13 says the "latest" check was deferred to cycle 0.5. As of today the answer is no.

**2. TOML v1.0.0 → v1.1.0 (nitpick-parse, FORMATS.md:15).**
- toml.io lists v1.1.0 first under "Full spec", and GitHub marks it "Latest", released "24 Dec". The year is not shown on the page. Inferred: 24 Dec 2025.
- What it could change: FORMATS.md:21 says that TOML without a version is "three incompatible languages". The 0.6 TOML cycle and its toml-test corpus are pinned to 1.0.0, so a conformance claim to "TOML" would now mean a non-current version. I did not diff 1.0.0 against 1.1.0.
- Source: https://toml.io/en/ and https://github.com/toml-lang/toml/releases

**3. POSIX.1-2017 → POSIX.1-2024, Issue 8 (nitpick-posix).**
- nitpick-time already pins Issue 8 for `TZ`, so the two repos cite different editions.
- What it could change: the XCU utility list and the XBD §12.2 guideline text that ARGUMENTS.md reproduces. I did not compare the editions.
- Source: https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/
