# POSIX.1-2017 against POSIX.1-2024 for nitpick-posix: a research digest, 2026-10-15

**As of 2026-10-15.** Question (the workbench's Q-1): which POSIX edition should `nitpick-posix` implement? Its documents pin POSIX.1-2017, Issue 7. The currency audit of 2026-10-09 marked that pin SUPERSEDED by POSIX.1-2024, Issue 8, without comparing the two. This digest makes that comparison, limited to what `nitpick-posix` implements. It decides nothing.

**Tags.** **[R]** means read in a primary source, or in this ecosystem's files, at the URL or line given. **[I]** means inferred from what I read and not stated by the source. Every URL below was read on **2026-10-15**.

**How the sources were read.** All web reading went through a fetch tool. Its model summarises pages and caps each verbatim quote at 125 characters. So most per-utility entries below are **close paraphrases of the CHANGE HISTORY lines, not full quotes**, though the defect numbers are exact. "Issue 8 change history" means the "Issue 8" subsection of each page's CHANGE HISTORY, at `https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/utilities/<name>.html`.

---

## Answer

The Open Group today serves **"The Open Group Base Specifications Issue 8", "IEEE Std 1003.1™-2024 Edition"**. Its preface says that "POSIX.1-2024 is simultaneously IEEE Std 1003.1™-2024 and The Open Group Standard Base Specifications, Issue 8". The archived 2018 edition, which is the one `nitpick-posix` pins, carries the banner "A newer edition of this standard exists". There is **no technical corrigendum to Issue 8**. The Austin Group says only that it is "currently working on maintenance of the 2024 edition". It also reports that ISO/IEC/IEEE 9945:2026 was published in March 2026.

Within `nitpick-posix`'s scope, Issue 8 changes three things:

- **The utility list.** Twelve utilities are removed (`fort77` and the eleven batch-queue `q*` utilities), seven are added (`gettext`, `msgfmt`, `ngettext`, `readlink`, `realpath`, `timeout`, `xgettext`), and `c99` becomes `c17`. That takes the list from 160 to 155.
- **The working set.** About 25 of the 98 standard utilities in the working set gain options or change behaviour. The largest change is that **`test`'s `-a`, `-o`, `(` and `)` are removed**, and `-ef`, `-nt`, `-ot`, `<` and `>` are added.
- **Extended regular expressions.** They gain a **minimal-repetition modifier** (`a*?`) and `REG_MINIMAL`.

**The Utility Syntax Guidelines (XBD §12.2) read the same in both editions, and there are fourteen of them in both.** `ARGUMENTS.md:11`'s "twelve" is wrong under either pin.

## 2. Each utility `nitpick-posix` implements: Issue 7 → Issue 8

**What I read.** The working set is `SCOPE.md` §3 (`meta/specs/SCOPE.md:115-128`): 100 utilities, of which `rev` and `clear` are extensions with no POSIX page. For each of the other **98**, I read the Issue 8 change history at the page URL above. All 98 pages have an Issue 8 subsection.

**Defects that appear on almost every page.** Two defects recur so often that they are listed here once rather than in every row:

- **Defect 1122** changes the description of `NLSPATH`. It is on almost every page.
- **Defect 251** "encourag[es] implementations to report an error" when a pathname containing a newline byte is displayed in a newline-separated format, and/or "to disallow the creation of filenames" containing one. It is on about 40 pages.

**[I]** 1122 is inert here, because `nitpick-posix` has no message catalogues (`SCOPE.md` C-7). 251 is a "should" that falls on `FILESYSTEM.md`'s "filenames that are not text". I did not read 251's normative text.

**Exit-status changes I did not read.** Many rows say only "changes the EXIT STATUS section", for example `false` 1321, `sleep` 1509, `uniq` 1492, `grep` 1502, `cp`/`mv`/`rm` 1732, `ln` 1506, `find` 1501, `kill` 1504, `expr` 1500, `stty` 1508, `diff` 1498 and `at` 1495. **I did not read what each new section says.** That is a gap for `SAFETY.md` S-12, which sets the status sets.

## 3. What Issue 8 adds that `nitpick-posix`'s scope touches [R unless marked]

**The utility list changes.** Source: https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/xrat/V4_xcu_chap01.html §C.1.1 and §C.3.

- **New in Issue 8:** "gettext, msgfmt, ngettext, readlink, realpath, timeout, xgettext".
- **Removed:** "fort77, qalter, qdel, qhold, qmove, qmsg, qrerun, qrls, qselect, qsig, qstat, qsub".
- C.3.3 names `c17` as the one addition to the 1992 base set.
- The 2018 index lists `c99` and `fort77`. The Issue 8 index lists `c17` and neither of those.
- Counting both indexes: **160 utilities in the 2018 edition and 155 in Issue 8** (https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9699919799.2018edition/idx/utilities.html and https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/idx/utilities.html).

**[I] What the list change means for `nitpick-posix`'s classes:**
- **`readlink`, `realpath` and `timeout`** are small standalone programs. None is in `SCOPE.md`, so under Issue 8 each must be built or placed in an exclusion class. `timeout` resembles the process group (0.8), and the other two resemble the filesystem group (0.6).
- **`gettext`, `msgfmt`, `ngettext` and `xgettext`** fit PX-007's locale class.
- **The batch-queue group** (`SCOPE.md` §2.3, `CONFORMANCE.md` §2's "batch queues | 11") **stops being a departure at all**, because those utilities are no longer in the standard.
- **PX-005's toolchain class** must name `c17` instead of `c99`, and drop `fort77`.

**Guidelines and syntax (XBD §12.2).** I read https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/basedefs/V1_chap12.html and https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9699919799.2018edition/basedefs/V1_chap12.html.

- **Both editions have 14 guidelines, and the wording the tool quoted is the same in each.** The only difference it showed is a cross-reference: "12.1 Utility Argument Syntax, item (2)" in Issue 8 against "Utility Argument Syntax, item (2)" in 2018.
- **[I]** `ARGUMENTS.md` G-1…G-10 cover Guidelines 1–14 between them. The count "twelve" (`ARGUMENTS.md:11`, `ROADMAP.md:77`) is wrong under **either** pin.
- **[U]** I did not compare §12.1 as a whole, and neither page has a change history.

**Regular expressions (XBD chapter 9), which reach `grep -E`, `sed -E`, `awk` and `nregex`:**
- Issue 8 §9.4.6 item 6: duplication symbols "can be suffixed by the repetition modifier '?'", which changes matching to "the leftmost shortest possible match". It also defines `REG_MINIMAL` (https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/basedefs/V1_chap09.html).
- The 2018 edition instead says: "The behavior of multiple adjacent duplication symbols … produces undefined results" (https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9699919799.2018edition/basedefs/V1_chap09.html).
- **Back-references remain BRE-only in Issue 8.** "Applicable only to basic regular expressions." So the documented `grep` back-reference departure (RX-102) is unchanged.
- **[I]** Under Issue 8, `grep -E 'a*?'` has defined behaviour that `nregex` would have to provide. Otherwise it becomes a new departure. That is a question for `nitpick-regex`, which this digest does not decide.

**Intrinsic utilities (XCU §1.7).** I read https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/utilities/V3_chap01.html.

- Issue 8 lists sixteen intrinsic utilities. They are **exactly `SCOPE.md` C-4's fifteen shell built-ins plus `kill`**.
- §1.6 still requires every intrinsic utility "except for kill" to be exempt from exec. In other words, `kill` must stay exec-able.
- **[I]** C-4's reasoning now has a normative name in Issue 8. `kill` stays buildable as a program, but under Issue 8 a shell will never find it through `PATH`.

**`strftime` (XSH), which `date` now defers to.** I read https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/functions/strftime.html.

- New in Issue 8: **`%s`**, seconds since the Epoch (169, 1386, 1612), and `%Ob`/`%OB` (258, 1166).
- Changed: `%C` (472), `%F` (739) and `%r` (1307). `tm_gmtoff` and `tm_zone` are added (1533).
- **[I]** Under Issue 8, `date +%s` is standard. Under the 2017 pin, `date`'s own list governs. `date`'s `%` grammar (SCOPE C-9, TM-104) therefore depends on the choice.

**The shell command language.** `sh` is excluded (PX-004), so I read nothing beyond XRAT's table of contents. That table lists a new "Dollar-Single-Quotes" section (C.2.2). Issue 8 also makes `time` a reserved word (`time` page, 267). **[I]** Neither touches a standalone utility.

## 5. Tradeoffs

**Keeping POSIX.1-2017:**
- *Work.* No edits to the edition sites. PX-002 stands. The catalogue check diffs against a fixed archive (the 160-utility index above).
- *Tests.* PX-022's conformance citations point at the archived 2018-edition URLs. **[I]** Differential references (GNU coreutils and BusyBox, PX-021) already ship several Issue 8 features, such as `head -c`, `find -print0`, `xargs -0` and `sed -E`. If `nitpick-posix` adds them, each one is a stated extension (C-2). If it leaves them out, each one is a mismatch against the reference.
- *Commitment.* `test -a`/`-o`/`()` stay in the standard as obsolescent and optional, per the Issue 8 page's description of their former status; I did not read the 2017 text. EREs keep `*?` undefined. `date`'s format list is its own.
- *Ecosystem.* The project conforms to an edition its publisher marks as superseded, while `nitpick-time` cites Issue 8. **[I]** At some later point the project would pay for the migration anyway, after code and cases exist.

**Moving to POSIX.1-2024:**
- *Work, in documents.*
  - A new PX decision superseding PX-002's edition clause, with the eight edition sites edited.
  - `SCOPE.md`'s classes revised: the batch group leaves the standard, `c99` becomes `c17`, and `fort77` goes.
  - A placement decision for each of `readlink`, `realpath` and `timeout`, and the `gettext` family assigned to PX-007.
  - `CONFORMANCE.md` §5's `head -c` line corrected.
- *Work, in cycles.* The options in §2.1 join the cycles that build those utilities: 0.3 `head -c` and `tail -r`; 0.6 `rm -d -v`, `find -print0 -iname -mount`, `ls` sockets; 0.8 `xargs -0 -r`, `ps -w`; 0.9 the `test` changes; 0.10 `sed -E` and `s///i`; 0.11 `awk` `nextfile`/`fflush` and the comparison rules; 0.12 `stty rows/cols/size`; `printf %n$`; `logger -f -i -p -t`.
- *Decision points.* `test`'s removed `-a`/`-o`/`()` becomes a choice between refusing them and keeping them as an extension. `grep -E` and `sed -E` raise the ERE minimal-repetition question for `nregex`.
- *Tests.* Citations point at the live `9799919799` URLs. **[I]** These may move if a corrigendum lands, because the Austin Group says maintenance is under way.
- *Commitment.* The current IEEE and Open Group edition, also published as ISO/IEC/IEEE 9945:2026. It matches `nitpick-time`.
- **[I]** Nothing in `nitpick-posix` is built yet (the brief's §1: probes only, no utility), so today the cost is almost entirely documents.

**Recommendation, for the author, who decides.** Pin **POSIX.1-2024**: The Open Group Base Specifications Issue 8, IEEE Std 1003.1-2024, served at `pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/`. Record it as a new PX decision superseding PX-002's edition clause, and add a `CURRENCY.md` row dated 2026-10-15.

The grounds:
- It is the edition The Open Group serves and ISO has adopted. The 2017 page itself says a newer edition exists.
- `nitpick-time` already cites it, so `date` would otherwise run under two editions.
- Cycle 0.0.1 has not run and no utility exists, so today the switch costs document edits rather than code.
- The changes it brings, such as `head -c`, `find -print0`, `xargs -0`, `sed -E` and `test -nt`, are mostly ones the differential references already have.
